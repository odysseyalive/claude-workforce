# Human Markers Assessment

**Before requiring changes, assess positive signals.** A piece with strong human markers and a few rhetorical devices is human writing, not AI writing. The goal is "sounds like a person thinking," not "doesn't sound like AI."

| Marker | What to Look For |
|--------|-----------------|
| **First-person presence** | "I" in the first few paragraphs, recurring naturally. Author is present, not observing from nowhere. |
| **Lived experience** | Specific sensory details, concrete provenance, named places/people/events the author encountered. |
| **Genuine commitment** | Author takes clear positions, not hedging everything. Says "I don't know" when uncertain rather than performing balance. |
| **Irregular rhythm** | Sentence lengths vary wildly — very short mixed with very long. Paragraph lengths respond to rhetorical needs, not templates. |
| **Texture words** | Conversational filler that AI strips: "other," "too," "also," "actually," "just," "really." |
| **Fragments and asides** | Sentence fragments used for emphasis. Parenthetical thoughts. Mid-sentence pivots. Note: fragment-answer pairs ("And the air? Thick with something intangible.") are a cultivated publishing-voice tool, not a diagnostic of authenticity. Their absence is not a defect and does not reduce the human-markers count. |
| **Contractions** | Natural use of "didn't," "wasn't," "I'd," "they're" rather than formal alternatives. |
| **Functional wit** | Humor that advances the argument, deadpan absurdity, self-implicating observations. Well-placed wit is a strong human tell because AI tends to avoid it or force it. |

**Over-correction warning:** When a piece scores strongly on 4+ human markers AND gets flagged for multiple AI patterns, the evaluator must surface this warning: *"This piece has strong voice markers. Review flags for false positives before requiring changes."* This protects against stripping authentic voice in pursuit of AI-pattern removal.
