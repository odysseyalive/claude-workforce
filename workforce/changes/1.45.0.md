Six jurors, yes or no, and the judge can finally see the ask (v1.45.0)

ASKED FOR by the user: "maybe the scoring is just yes or no. A jury is traditionally
made up of 12 people. And all 12 people have to say yes or no. It's not any more
complicated than that ... here in the sensory agency, you're just dealing with six."
And the reason it matters: "what's more important, scoring a number or getting
inspired by agents who give you new ideas and things to work on ... if it's a no, you
will probably get a reason why you got the no. And that also helps widen the
perspective before you even run the Widener."

THE VERDICT IS A VOTE, NOT A NUMBER. Each juror answers one question about the
finished turn and one verified `no` is a `no`. A juror may ABSTAIN — a vote nobody
could check is the fabricated number in another costume — and notes on a `yes` are
explicitly welcome, because a verdict says you were wrong while a note says where to
look, and the second is the one that widens. "You never read the record that removed
it" IS the widening, before any widening process runs.

A JURY COSTS ABOUT WHAT ONE OPINION DID, which is why this is affordable. The docket
is `ask.senses`, and enumerating every trigger and task kind that ships: applicable
senses per turn is 0, 1 or 2 in every case. Most turns seat one or two jurors, not six.

THE SESSION NO LONGER DECIDES WHO SITS. That was the last place it graded itself: the
0-100 went in 1.44.0 but `wf-widen` still chose whether to convene, from its own read
of its own work. The docket now comes from the ASK, classified from the user's words
before any answer existed.

AND THE BUG UNDERNEATH ALL OF IT: `Stop` HAS NO `prompt` FIELD. Confirmed against the
hooks reference. So `payload.get("prompt")` at Stop returned an empty string every
time, and every flag written since 1.44.0 carried `ask.prompt: ""`. **The judge had
never seen the ask.** The measure-results directive was implemented with the request
missing — comparing evidence to conclusion with no idea what was wanted. Found by
testing a draft jury prompt against a real flag file, not by reading the code, which
I had read a dozen times that hour while writing commit messages about measuring
results against the request. The prompt and the docket are carried forward from
`wf-task-tag`, which runs on `UserPromptSubmit`, the one event that receives them.

THE SIXTH JUROR CAN NOW SIT. `intuition` shipped permanently unscored on the argument
that judging one's own prior from inside is the impaired faculty — true, and it only
ever ruled out SELF-judgement. A juror did not form the prior and can read it.

abstain option rather than a single global verdict.
