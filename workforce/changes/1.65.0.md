A split author owns its handbook and its scratch, and proves retention against
the right snapshot

DEF-Q-012, reported from a live Step 5d split and measured twice in one run.
A dispatched author was granted no exclusive claim on the handbook it was
relocating and no private scratch directory, so: (1) two authors each wrote
`original.md` into the shared scratch ROOT and one then extracted the OTHER
handbook's text into its grounding files; (2) the principal edited a handbook
while its author was mid-round, having read an idle `ListAgents` row as
finished. Both were caught on read-back because both agents verified rather
than trusted, and both still left duplicate relocations of two blocks in two
grounding files. Residue is a defect in its own right (§ Directives,
2026-07-30), so "nothing was lost" closes neither.

THE THIRD LINE IS WHY THIS IS A RULE AND NOT A NOTE. A retention proof asks
whether every line of the before-text survives in the handbook plus its
grounding library. Run against a snapshot of a DIFFERENT handbook that question
is still well-formed and still answerable — it proves the wrong thing, cleanly,
with no error anywhere. A contaminated snapshot passes every containment check
that can be run against it, so the proof succeeds in exactly the case it exists
to catch. The `name:` frontmatter is the cheapest mechanical discriminator,
which is why the brief carries a `grep -q` rather than an instruction to be
careful.

The liveness rule directly below was half of this already and could not reach
it: it says an idle or empty `ListAgents` never proves an author is DEAD, and
never said the same listing is equally worthless for concluding it is safe to
TOUCH the file. That is the gap failure (2) fell through.

ALSO FIXED, AND IT IS MINE: 1.64.3 corrected the length floor to keep each
relocated section's residue, and left `procedure-for-procedures.md` describing
the zeroing behaviour it had just removed — including the stale 181, in the
paragraph that tells a reader when the split is ordered. That is precisely the
lesson this project recorded on 2026-09-11 ("a commit that removes a mechanism
may not also be the commit that asserts the mechanism survives elsewhere"),
landing on the commit that cites it. Both passages now state the residue rule
and the corrected 189.

Proven by making it fail: the three brief lines were removed and the new check
fired by name. 1401 checks pass.
