A guard that fires on 7% of good replies teaches you to ignore it (v1.30.1)

`wf-speak-guard` had a clause flagging a reply that named defects and stated no
outcome for any of them. Written 2026-09-07 against a real complaint, and too
coarse to keep: `_FINDING` matched ordinary words — "wrong", "failed", "gap" —
`_DISPOSITION` cleared on "now ", "already", "added", and the pairing it actually
wanted, THIS finding to THIS outcome, is a judgment no substring test reaches.

MEASURED over 2,149 real assistant replies from this machine's own transcripts:
it fired on 151 of them, 7.0%. The samples are dispatch announcements and status
notes with nothing unresolved in them — "Pages are live and fresh", a routing
decision, a verification that had just finished. It also fired twice inside one
hour on clean replies in the session that removed it, the second time on a
completion report listing no problems at all.

That is the failure mode this project already names for itself: a checker firing
on good output does not teach a model to write better, it teaches a reader to
stop reading the checker. Removed, and the removal is asserted so it stays
removed — re-adding a substring test for this would repeat a measured failure.
The rule is unchanged and carried where it always was, by
`references/plain-output.md` § Say the state, not the journey and by the output
style, which is read at full strength on every turn.

The guard's remaining reach on the same corpus: 1.3% blocked (opener and
hand-back, both closed-vocabulary and position-anchored), 2.4% noted for a buried
question. `speakguard-undisposed` is kept as a fixture with its expectation
inverted — the reply that used to be flagged must now pass silently.

bin/check 1263 passed, 0 failed. Fifteen guard and brief fixtures green.
