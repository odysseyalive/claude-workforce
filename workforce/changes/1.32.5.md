The guard against unproven cases had been cancelled by a wildcard

The rerun came back 706 of 724 with 15 vacuous — `delall` cleared 39 of the 54,
as intended. Reading the 15 found the reason ten of them existed, and it is
worse than ten weak assertions.

`prove: every case name is exactly the name of the check it breaks` was GREEN
while ten prove cases named assertions bin/check no longer has. Two separate
blindnesses, both in the same block:

It read BOTH files with a regex. A `check(` call indented inside an `if` — how a
skipped pair is written — is invisible to a pattern anchored on `\ncheck(`, and
a case tuple laid out any way but name-alone-on-its-own-line is invisible to the
case pattern. Both sides now come from the syntax tree.

Worse, its f-string allowance was a scrape of every f-string in the file, which
harvests DETAIL arguments too — they look identical. One of them was a bare
interpolation, and as a pattern it compiled to `^.*$`: a wildcard matching every
case name, so the guard reported zero orphans no matter what bin/prove held. A
wildcard built from an unfiltered scrape does not widen a check, it cancels it.
f-string names now come from `check(`'s first argument, and one carrying no
fixed words is dropped rather than compiled into a blanket match.

Ten orphan cases removed. The other five vacuous verdicts:

One was a case deleting more text than its assertion tests — the check reads
`"BARE_WF_RX" in _c6` and the case removed only the definition line, so the
substring survived elsewhere in the file. Now a delall on the tested substring.

Three were `A or B` assertions, which no single mutation can falsify: delete A
and B still holds. The generator no longer emits a case for a disjunction. A
case that is vacuous by construction is worse than none — it reports an
assertion as weak when the truth is that this tool cannot express its mutation.
They join the structural remainder the summary already counts.

The fifth was the orphan guard itself, and it now fires: proven by deleting the
literal it names.
