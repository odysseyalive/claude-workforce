The stop and the report were the same act (v1.39.0)

ASKED FOR by the user, twice, and the second time as a defect in its own right:
"This stopping for no apparent reason has to get fixed. What's going on here?"
Then: "you need to build it into the workflow. You don't stop right in the middle
of what you're working on. Unless you actually really do need input."

MEASURED before anything was built, over the 106 turns of the session that built
it. 12 turns (11.3%) ended with a tail promising more work. 10 (9.4%) promised
more work and asked nothing at all. Every stop_reason in the transcript is
`end_turn` — no token cap, no harness interrupt, no tool error. Nothing stopped
those turns. The turn stopped.

THE CAUSE IS STRUCTURAL, not a lapse of will. There is no way to say something to
the user and keep working: a text block with no tool call after it IS the end of
the turn. So the sentence "Building it." does not describe what happens next — it
is the act of stopping, spelled as a promise not to. From inside it reads as
narrating progress, which is exactly why noticing never caught it, and it lands
hardest right after a long complete-feeling report, because completeness feels
terminal.

Of the 10, one is a correct stop — "nothing to do but let it finish. Continuing
to wait" — a turn waiting on a background job. The Stop payload carries
`background_tasks`, so that exemption is read from data rather than guessed from
a phrase. False-positive rate on good replies: 1 in 106, 0.9%, against the 7.0%
that retired this distribution's last speech guard.

`wf-widen` — THE ACTING HALF THE LEDGER LEFT UNDONE. The ledger's own header says
the operator has to decide to act on its report, "and that decision is made from
inside the tunnel." It concluded self-checks cannot work and stopped there. But
the same measurement says three of ten escapes came from a spawned panel, and a
spawn is NOT self-checking: fresh context, no history, none of the reasoning that
built the tunnel. What kept it from firing was never that it does not work — it
was that spawning required a decision made from inside. So this fires the outside
automatically, at `Stop`, where the harness rather than the operator decides.

THE SIX SENSES, which the user named across the session — three muses, then the
three monkeys, then six with intuition as the sixth — are what the score is made
of, and `references/senses.md` is the guideline. SIGHT reads the thing. HEARING
is testimony: a spawned reader, the web, a skill. TOUCH runs it. TASTE
enumerates. SMELL selects which sense applies and never scores. INTUITION is
deliberately unscored, because scoring a prior means judging it, and judging
one's own prior from inside is the faculty the measurement found impaired.

THE DENOMINATOR MOVES, and that is the design rather than a shortcut. "Not every
situation needs all six" — the user, and they are right: a turn scored out of a
fixed six is marked down for failing to spawn a reader when spawning one was
pointless, and a guard that marks down correct work is the guard that gets
ignored. A turn is scored only on the senses its claim needs. A turn tripping
nothing prints "not scored" rather than a fake 100/100.

TWO MAPPINGS DELIBERATELY NOT SHIPPED. A claim about what a SCRIPT DOES is
settled by touch; a claim about the WORLD OUTSIDE this repo is settled by
hearing. Both are obviously right and both need a detector, and a detector ships
with a measured false-positive rate over real traffic or it does not ship. The
two that are live were measured at 0.5% and 1.5% over 1,495 turns.

reads the tree and reports what ships, deriving the hook set from
