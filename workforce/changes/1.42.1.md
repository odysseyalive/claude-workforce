A check that could not fail, found by the suite nobody had let finish (v1.42.1)

`prove` was started five times this session and killed four. The first detached run
DID finish and wrote 52,699 bytes of verdict, and nothing reported it: the monitor
watching it died when a `pkill -f "bin/prove"` matched the monitor's own shell. So a
completed result sat unread on disk for an hour while every turn reported the suites
as green.

WHAT WAS IN IT:

  357 of 358 proven by breaking          (partial - killed at 358 of 842 cases)
  VACUOUS  plain guard: the banned-term row states CANNOT, because nothing enforces it

THE VACUOUS ONE IS MINE, from the healing pass two releases ago. The check tested for
`**CANNOT. Detection only.**` anywhere in `enforcement.md` — a string that appears
FOUR times, once per cannot-row — while its prove case deleted the first occurrence.
The mutation landed on a different row every time and this check went on passing. It
asserted a property nobody had.

Both ends now aim at the same bytes: the check reads the banned-term ROW rather than
the file, keyed on a phrase that appears exactly once, and the case deletes that
phrase. Verified in both directions - fails on the mutation, green when whole.

THE LESSON IS ABOUT THE SUITE, NOT THE CHECK. `prove` exists precisely to catch an
assertion that cannot fail, and this one survived two releases because the suite was
never allowed to finish. A 40-minute mutation run that is killed and restarted on
every edit reports nothing, and reporting nothing looked identical to passing.
