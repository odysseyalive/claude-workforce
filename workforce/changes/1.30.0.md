Date the observation, do not grade the prose (v1.30.0)

A session told the user for four hours that a test suite was running. It had
read `ps` once, seen a pid, and repeated that reading in the present tense ever
since. Checked from another session: no suite process existed, the pid was a
sleep loop the session had started itself whose `pgrep` pattern matched its own
command line and so could never exit, and the browser processes belonged to an
unrelated tool. The same night it told him "There are two Spark accounts" from a
cached page captured half an hour before he added his card, then re-read the same
cache and called the agreement corroboration.

Both are one shape: an observation made at T, asserted at T+hours with its age
stripped off. Neither is a wording problem. "The suite is running" is a
well-formed sentence and it was false, so nothing that reads finished prose can
find it.

The first attempt did read finished prose — seven pattern clauses on `Stop`. Two
false-fired on good replies within twenty minutes and were hand-tuned, and the
user named the disease: "You are writing patches for something that is a systemic
issue. next time it will just be something else... there are infinite possible
feedbacks." A red-team run put numbers on it: over 400 real replies from this
machine's transcripts the seven blocked 12% and nudged 21%, roughly tripling the
veto rate, while two of them fired zero times. It also proved a wedge — one clause
blocked a draft and a second blocked the corrected retry at 100%, because the
rejected draft was still in the transcript. All seven are reverted.

What ships instead is a PRODUCER. `wf-turn-brief` runs on `UserPromptSubmit`,
before a word is written, and publishes what the session actually looked at with
each reading's age: live-state checks tagged as decaying in minutes, a repeated
check tagged `REPEAT xN` because running the same probe again is not a second
opinion. Actions are skipped — `git checkout`, `chmod`, a heredoc patch changed
something and observed nothing, and dating them buries the rows that bear on what
is still true. It cannot block and never judges a claim. It dates one. Verified
against the real transcript: the four-hour `ps` and the repeated page read both
appear. 0.40s on a 19MB transcript.

`wf-speak-guard` regains exactly one clause, as a measurement rather than a
ruling: the share of a message that repeats one already sent this turn, reported
as a number, never blocking. Jaccard, not overlap-over-the-smaller — the latter
scores a short closing summary of a long message at ~100%, and fired 24/400 on
progress notes. Measured over 1,802 real messages from 366 multi-message turns:
one fire, 0.06%, and it still catches the two near-identical replies from that
night at 59%.

Two rules ship as prose in the output style, the one carrier at full strength on
every turn, and are labelled advisory because nothing proves they fire. Before
calling anything broken, ask one OPEN question — "what is this behaviour?" — and
answer it with a command, not from memory; never a yes/no question, because a
model agrees with whatever a yes/no question assumes (Chain-of-Verification,
arXiv 2309.11495). And a recovered mistake is not a finding.

Two slots are left deliberately empty and say so. Pronoun ambiguity and the
one-claim-per-sentence discipline that GSAR (arXiv 2604.23366) names as the
defeater of any grounding partition are not computable from a transcript, and
every mechanical approximation is a curated word list that grows forever and
fires on good prose. An honestly empty slot beats a checker that has to be
apologised for.

Length is not a target anywhere in this change. An earlier draft made it one and
the user's correction was "I don't care how long the reply is back, I just want
it to be accurate and understandable, period." The defect in the 300-word answer
to his 12-word question was never its size — one of the three problems it
reported was not real.

Reaches existing installs the ordinary way: one `SHIPPED_HOOKS` row, so `install`,
`audit` step 6-H and `/workforce hooks --execute` all wire it. Confirmed against
three other projects on this machine. Four re-runnable fixtures; bin/check 1263
passed, 0 failed.
