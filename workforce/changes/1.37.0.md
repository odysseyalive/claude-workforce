The tunnel issue, worked directly instead of around. The question was never the

  reasoning-depth counter        fires NEVER

The depth counter is the interesting negative. The Deterministic Horizon (arXiv
2606.00376) puts unaided state-tracking accuracy below 50% at 19-31 steps, and
that paper reframed the whole problem — until it was measured here. Observed depth:
median 1, mean 1.3, MAX 5. Never within a factor of four of the horizon, and every
failing claim was written at depth 1 or 2, immediately after a read. So the failure
model was wrong: reasoning does not drift as it runs. A shallow read gets treated
as sufficient, and the wrong conclusion is immediate.

WHAT SHIPPED is a conjunction of two mechanical facts, neither a judgment: the turn
ran no read (a count), and the reply asserts absence about something the tree could
answer for (a closed vocabulary inside a noun window). Neither half works alone —
zero reads alone is 11.2% and usually CORRECT, since answering from context in hand
is directive two's own instruction.

The noun window reads BOTH directions, and that is not symmetry for its own sake.
Looking only forward silently dropped the very claim that started this work: "this
repo carries no org and does not need one" puts its nouns BEFORE the phrase. Caught
by testing against the three real failures rather than against invented examples.

Of the seven fires in 1,495 turns, ONE is the vendor claim a human had to correct.

IT REPORTS AND NEVER BLOCKS. StepShield (arXiv 2601.22136, NeurIPS 2026) measured

indistinguishable from random (EIR 0.23 vs 0.24, p=0.66), because "regex guardrails
detect syntax, not intent". This is a regex. 0.5% is cheap enough to print and
nowhere near good enough to gate a turn on.

Two fixtures: the trigger firing on the real claim, and the same claim NOT firing
